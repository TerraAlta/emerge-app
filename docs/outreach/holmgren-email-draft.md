## Notes on tone

- **200 words.** Short enough to read before breakfast. Long enough to be serious.
- **Credit first.** Two paragraphs of what Emerge is *and* how it uses the flower, *before* asking anything.
- **Two questions, not one.** The first is small (permission + phrasing). The second is bigger (real conversation). Framing the small one first gives a low-friction "yes" which makes the second easier.
- **"I'm not asking for endorsement."** Holmgren is asked for endorsements constantly. Explicitly naming what you're NOT asking builds trust.
- **The compass list is a signal.** He'll see you've thought about the broader lineage, not just him. That matters.
- **Soft close.** "Thank you for the flower. It's the map." is a warm line that doesn't oversell. Keep it.

## What to do BEFORE sending

1. Verify the onboarding card actually links to his site (it does — `https://holmgren.com.au/product/permaculture-flower-poster/`).
2. Click through your own app as an anon visitor and time the journey from landing to seeing the permaculture flower paragraph. Should be under 30 seconds. If it's not, fix that first.
3. Ideally: reply to one of his blog posts or mastodon / bluesky in the weeks before emailing, so your name isn't a total stranger.

## If he doesn't reply

- Don't follow up for at least 3 weeks. Elders get a lot of mail.
- Second follow-up can be a single sentence: *"Quick ping in case my April note was lost — no pressure."*
- If no reply after the second ping, leave it for six months. Build Emerge. Reply later with evidence of users, not asks.

## If he says yes to question 1 but not 2

That's already a win. Use the permission to put a small line on the landing page: *"Uses the permaculture flower framework by David Holmgren, used with permission."* That alone raises credibility hugely.
